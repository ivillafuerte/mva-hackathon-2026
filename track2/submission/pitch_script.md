Our question is whether restoring functional BUBR1 can reduce the generation of new abnormal cells in mosaic variegated aneuploidy.

The challenge nominated two BUB1B variants: L737 stop and N1002K. We reconstructed the premature-stop context and analyzed the actual human protein model. The stop is compatible with RNA decay. The missense variant may alter local contacts, but its instability is not proven. We do not turn structural predictions into experimental facts.

Our proposal uses normal BUBR1 as an experimental reference. We would measure how much protein is needed, how many cells must receive it, and whether early or delayed restoration changes new errors per division. We would then withdraw the rescue to test durability.

Approved medications are evaluated against that reference. Sirolimus is our leading downstream hypothesis, comparing transient exposure with sustained inhibition. Anakinra tests a more focused inflammatory mechanism. Nicotinic acid explores a lower-confidence NAD–SIRT2 route to functional BUBR1. These are separate hypotheses, not a proposed combination or a prescription.

A candidate must improve function at plausible exposure. Fewer micronuclei caused by cell death or stopped division would not count as correction. We also distinguish tissue protection from actual restoration of BUBR1.

Prenatal BUB1B mRNA is our future research direction. It could test prevention during development, but it is not an approved repurposed drug and is not our eligible intervention. We do not claim that it reverses existing aneuploidy or cancer.

Our contribution is reproducible allele analysis and a falsifiable framework for choosing interventions. The computational results are complete; the biological validation is proposed.

We are deeply grateful to the child and their family who generously contributed their data and their story to advance research into this rare disease. We acknowledge their trust in making this Hackathon possible.
